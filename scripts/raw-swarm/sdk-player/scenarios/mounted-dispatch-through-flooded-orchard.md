Flint-Knot, a Goblin Warrior mounted on a controlled Riding Horse, begins 80 feet west of a wooden gate at the far end of a flooded orchard lane. Flint-Knot possesses a synthetic brass dispatch cylinder and is seriously attempting to carry it through the gate. The cylinder is a Small 6-pound object strapped to Flint-Knot and remains with Flint-Knot after a fall or dismount. On its own turn, the carrier may drop it without an action or pass it to a willing creature within reach using a free object interaction. A creature within reach of the unattended cylinder may retrieve it with a free object interaction. It cannot be removed from a conscious unwilling carrier, opened, destroyed, or secured to another creature during the encounter. It has passed through the gate when its carrier enters the first space wholly east of the gate.

The player directs Cinder, an Imp, and Hook and Spur, two Goblin Warriors. Hook begins 45 feet behind the horse and 10 feet north of the lane. Spur begins 45 feet behind the horse and 10 feet south of the lane. Cinder begins flying 20 feet above Hook and may begin either visible or Invisible, as the player chooses. Hook and Spur are seriously attempting to keep the cylinder west of the gate. The player chooses whether they close for melee, use Shortbows, split around obstacles, or concentrate on the current carrier. Cinder is seriously attempting to disrupt the carrier or its escort while limiting exposure to attacks; the player chooses its movement, actions, targets, and whether it uses Invisibility. Cinder cannot carry or manipulate the cylinder.

Moss-Bite, a Wolf, begins adjacent to the horse on the side chosen by the GM. Moss-Bite is hostile to Hook, Spur, and Cinder and is seriously attempting to prevent any of them from blocking, disabling, or overtaking the cylinder carrier before the carrier passes through the gate. On its first turn, Moss-Bite moves to attack whichever visible pursuer has the shortest unobstructed route to the carrier, with ties chosen by the GM. On later turns, the GM chooses whether Moss-Bite attacks a creature within reach, pursues the hostile creature closest to the cylinder, or occupies a legal space adjacent to the carrier on the side from which the nearest visible pursuer is approaching. Moss-Bite cannot select Cinder by position while Cinder is Invisible unless Moss-Bite has located Cinder under the normal rules. Its tactic changes only on its own turn.

The central lane is 15 feet wide. A flooded strip begins 30 feet east of Flint-Knot and extends for 25 feet. The water is 3 feet deep and is Difficult Terrain for creatures moving along the ground. Two parallel rows of trees border the strip, leaving a dry northern detour that adds 15 feet to the route and a dry southern detour that adds 25 feet. Each tree trunk is a 5-foot-wide obstacle. Foliage within 10 feet of a trunk is Lightly Obscured, and a trunk grants Half Cover or Three-Quarters Cover only when it blocks the corresponding portion of the line between the specific attacker and target. Beyond the flooded strip, the last 25 feet to the gate are dry and unobstructed. Bright daylight illuminates the lane, and no creature other than a player-chosen Invisible Cinder begins unseen.

Flint-Knot initially directs the horse to Dash toward the flooded strip. At the start of each later Flint-Knot turn, the GM chooses a route and tactic in response to the pursuers’ actual positions. The horse may Dash through the water, Dash along either dry detour, or Disengage and move along any legal route; alternatively, Flint-Knot may attack a legal target while the horse moves without Dashing. The GM must seriously pursue passage of the cylinder rather than always repeat one route. If Flint-Knot is dismounted, Flint-Knot retains the cylinder and chooses a route toward the gate on foot. Flint-Knot may pass the cylinder to Moss-Bite while they are within reach. Moss-Bite then seriously attempts to carry it through the gate, while Flint-Knot attacks the pursuer with the shortest legal route to Moss-Bite or occupies a legal space that obstructs that route. If the cylinder is unattended, Flint-Knot and Moss-Bite decide independently on their own turns whether retrieving it better serves carrying it through the gate than engaging a pursuer.
