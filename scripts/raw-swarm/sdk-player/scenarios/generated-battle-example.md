Four 4th-level adventurers protect a visibly synthetic beacon crystal from a concentrated west-to-east breach through the end of round eight. Before placement, the players finalize four SRD-legal character sheets using only options exposed by the supplied public SDK, including equipment, ammunition, prepared options, and every choice affecting combat. They start at maximum Hit Points with all class resources, receive no magic items, and have no mounts, companions, or pre-summoned creatures.

Use 5-foot squares in columns 1–20 west to east and rows A–F north to south. The roofless chamber occupies columns 7–14 and rows B–E. Its opaque, 15-foot-high walls follow the outside edges except for a 10-foot-wide west doorway at 7C–7D and east doorway at 14C–14D. The entire map is brightly illuminated. A waist-high barricade occupies 8C–8D, and a second occupies 13C–13D. Barricade squares are difficult terrain, can be entered, and give an occupant half cover against attacks originating on the opposite side of that barricade; they do not require crouching or Prone. All other squares are clear ground, and there are no unplaced exterior covered positions.

The crystal occupies 11C. It is immovable, blocks movement through its square, does not block sight, and gives half cover when it intervenes between attacker and target. It has AC 15, 30 Hit Points, immunity to poison and psychic damage, and cannot receive creature-only conditions; all other suitable attacks and damaging effects can harm it normally.

The players place one adventurer in each of 10B, 10E, 12B, and 12E, deciding which completed character sheet occupies each square. Two Goblin Warriors begin in 2C and 2D, Wolves begin in 3B and 3E, and two more Goblin Warriors begin in 5C and 5D. No combatant begins hidden. Both sides know the chamber layout, the crystal’s properties, and all visible enemies; nobody is Surprised, and Initiative is rolled normally.

The attackers begin with a serious plan to create a firing lane rather than feed combatants through the doorway singly. Goblin Warriors seek covered or distant positions from which they can pressure the crystal or exposed defenders. The Wolves approach by different sides of the west doorway and try to open a route toward the crystal while threatening defenders who block it. The attackers may adapt their movement, targets, use of Nimble Escape, and coordination as the defenders’ formation and the battle state change. The Wolves’ attacks and positioning do not immobilize a defender except through an effect explicitly supplied by their stat block or the normal rules.

The players freely determine whether to hold the barricades, counterattack through the west door, establish ranged crossfire, or keep a mobile reserve; their strategy may likewise change after observing play. No tactical phrase changes the rules for cover, hiding, visibility, or movement.

The attackers achieve their objective as soon as the crystal reaches 0 Hit Points. The defenders achieve theirs if the attacking force is defeated under the normal combat-ending rules, or if the crystal retains at least 1 Hit Point at the end of round eight while combat remains underway. If the defending force is defeated first, the attackers win.
