Create a 60-foot-long by 40-foot-wide, brightly illuminated courtyard with a flat floor at altitude 0 and open air to an arena boundary 30 feet above the floor. Coordinates are measured from the courtyard center. Place two solid square pillars with horizontal footprints from (-5, -20) to (5, -10) and from (-5, 10) to (5, 20); each rises from the floor through the upper arena boundary, blocks passage and lines through its footprint, and cannot be entered. Place a Goblin Warrior centered at (-25, 15), a Skeleton at (25, 15), a Wolf at (-25, -15), and a Riding Horse at (25, -15), all on the floor. The Goblin Warrior is hostile to the Skeleton and Wolf and noncooperative with the Riding Horse. The Skeleton is hostile to the Goblin Warrior and Riding Horse and noncooperative with the Wolf. The Wolf is hostile to the Goblin Warrior and Riding Horse. The Riding Horse is hostile to the Skeleton and Wolf.

The Goblin Warrior’s objective is to attack the Skeleton while ending movement with a pillar between itself and the Wolf. It redirects to the Wolf when the Wolf begins within 10 feet or has attacked it since its previous turn. The Skeleton advances on the Riding Horse and prefers an endpoint that leaves the Goblin Warrior without an unobstructed line to it. It redirects to the Goblin Warrior if the Goblin Warrior has attacked it since its previous turn and is no farther away than the Riding Horse. The Wolf pursues the Riding Horse, choosing a legal route that passes on the side of the southern pillar farther from the Goblin Warrior; it redirects to the Goblin Warrior whenever that creature begins within the Wolf’s available movement distance and the Riding Horse cannot be legally attacked after that movement. The Riding Horse seeks a legal attack against the Wolf but redirects to the Skeleton if the Skeleton begins within 5 feet or has attacked it since its previous turn.

Run three rounds, allowing each active player to choose among surfaced attacks, targets, and legal routes consistent with that combatant’s current priority. Geometry owns placements and determines distances, reach, lines, cover, pillar collision, route length, and which side of a pillar a route traverses. Resolve every surfaced movement reaction or interrupt for which the reacting creature can legally target the mover and its current objective supports doing so; if multiple surfaced responses remain, leave that choice to the reacting player. Resolve attacks, rolls, damage, movement, and interrupts through the public player operations, and complete every active turn with End Turn.
