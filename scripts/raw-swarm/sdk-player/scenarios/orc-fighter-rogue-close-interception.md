Create two fresh level-1 Orc Character Sheets specialized for a close interception. The Sage Fighter assigns Strength 15, Constitution 14, Dexterity 13, Intelligence 12, Wisdom 10, Charisma 8, then applies +2 Constitution and +1 Intelligence. Select the surfaced Defense Fighting Style, the surfaced Weapon Mastery option matching the selected one-handed melee weapon, and the supported initial loadout with maximum Armor Class; a tied choice must use a donned shield. The Acolyte Rogue assigns Dexterity 15, Wisdom 14, Constitution 13, Intelligence 12, Charisma 10, Strength 8, then applies +2 Wisdom and +1 Intelligence. Select supported armor, a usable melee weapon, a surfaced ranged weapon with ammunition, the matching surfaced Weapon Mastery choice, and surfaced Expertise favoring Perception and Stealth. The controller completes all proficiency, language, Magic Initiate cantrip, level-1 spell, and spellcasting-ability holes, choosing combat-capable surfaced spells; the Fighter favors reliable damage, and the Rogue favors healing or protection. Both sheets begin fresh at full resources with no conditions or expended state.

Use static stat-block damage in a brightly illuminated 40-by-40-foot enclosed yard on a five-foot grid. Coordinates identify southwest square corners, with 0 ≤ x < 40 and 0 ≤ y < 40. Place the Fighter at (10,15), the Rogue at (10,20), Wolf A at (25,10), Wolf B at (25,25), and Goblin Warrior A at (30,20). Place open waist-high racks at (20,15) and (20,20). Their squares are traversable at normal cost and give Half Cover exactly when the canonical line between source and target crosses one of those squares. They do not block sight. Every combatant and the objective is initially visible to every other combatant, and all remaining squares are open with no Cover.

Anchor a visibly synthetic glass calibration prism at (35,20). Its five-foot footprint does not occupy creature space, impede movement, block sight, or provide Cover. The prism has AC 12, 16 Hit Points, no damage threshold or damage adjustments, and only the canonical ordinary object immunity to Poison and Psychic damage. It begins undamaged.

Use fixed stat-block Hit Points. Wolf A, Wolf B, and Goblin Warrior A begin at full Hit Points, unsurprised, visible, free of conditions, and with every action, Bonus Action, Reaction, movement resource, and limited use unspent. The Wolves use their canonical natural melee attacks and require no ammunition. Goblin Warrior A wears the armor and uses any shield represented by its canonical Armor Class, begins ready to make its canonical ranged attack with 20 pieces of ammunition, and retains its canonical melee attack. Roll and retain Initiative for all five combatants.

Record movement-ally relationships only from Wolf A to Wolf B, Wolf B to Wolf A, Fighter to Rogue, and Rogue to Fighter. Goblin Warrior A has no movement-ally relationship. Record initial ranged-attack enemy relationships only from Goblin Warrior A to the Fighter and Rogue and from the Rogue to Wolf A, Wolf B, and Goblin Warrior A. Record ordinary Opportunity Attack enemy relationships in both directions for each pairing of the Fighter or Rogue with Wolf A, Wolf B, or Goblin Warrior A. Do not infer additional relationships.

Wolf A advances by the shortest open route toward the calibration prism. Wolf B first moves toward the Rogue and uses its canonical melee attack against the Rogue whenever available; if the Rogue cannot be reached with current movement, Wolf B moves toward the prism instead. A Wolf adjacent to a Character Sheet attacks that sheet; otherwise it continues toward the prism, and when adjacent to the prism it attacks the object. Goblin Warrior A initially makes a ranged attack against the Fighter. On later turns it uses melee against an adjacent Character Sheet; otherwise it attacks the Character Sheet nearest to either Wolf, breaking ties in favor of the Rogue. If no supported attack is available, it moves along an open route that reduces its distance to the nearer Character Sheet. It never attempts a ranged attack after exhausting its ammunition.

The Fighter and Rogue try to keep the prism above 0 Hit Points while attacking the named approaching combatants. Their controller decides whether the Fighter blocks Wolf A or Wolf B and whether the Rogue supports that engagement, pressures Goblin Warrior A, or attacks the unengaged Wolf at range. The controller reevaluates using current distances, occupied-space traversal, Cover, Hit Points, remaining ammunition, available acts, and prism damage. The Table treats play as concluded when the prism reaches 0 Hit Points; when Wolf A, Wolf B, and Goblin Warrior A are each terminally dead; or when both Character Sheets are terminally dead or unable to take another supported battle act.
